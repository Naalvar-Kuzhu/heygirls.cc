A tiny thought about language

Hey girls.

What if the way we address a group didn't quietly assume everyone's gender?

SCROLL TO EXPLORE ↓
01 — The problem

“Hey guys” somehow became everyone.

We use it for groups of men. Groups of women. Groups of everyone.

But when a space is predominantly girls, the alternative often becomes “girlies,” “girls,” or “gals.”

It sounds cute. It sounds friendly.

But it also assumes everyone in the room identifies as a girl.

And that's the weird part.

One gendered word is treated as universal, while another is treated as specific.
02 — The question

Why should the language we use to make people feel included depend on their gender?

03 — The solution

Just say something else.

Instead of defaulting to gendered language, there are plenty of words that welcome everyone.

everyone
everybody
folks
all
friends
team
y'all

04 — The point

It's not about policing language.

It's not about banning “girlies.”

It's simply about making room for everyone.

Language evolves because people use it. This is just a little nudge toward using words that don't make inclusion dependent on gender.
